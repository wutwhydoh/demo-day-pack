> Awesome. So I am a director of marketing at demand drive. We're a go to market agency. We work with a variety of different B2B orgs — pretty staunchly a tech enabled, human powered organization. Not just for our internal efforts, but how we build out our programs for clients, we firmly believe that human operators have to sit at the core, and they're best enabled and not replaced by tech. We look at that through the lens of what I call the three C's: that's capture, contextualize and connect. A lot of teams start and end at capture. They take signals — like what we're getting in sift here: companies that are visiting our website, what pages they go to — and they just dump that in the lap of their sales team and say, hey, have at it. We struggled with that prior issue of efficiency, of the idea of capturing these leads. Before we had sift, [we had] a couple of different tools that allowed us to see what companies were on our site; getting that relevant data piped to our reps was manual, slow, inconsistent, and by the time they saw the signal, the moment for action had passed. For us, the big unlock here — beyond the fact that this isn't manual anymore; we reduced the enrichment effort by over 95%, cut lag time from like 48 hours down to real time — is that sift also does the filtering work for you. So reps can focus on the accounts that actually move the needle the most. This is a motion — or a profile — that I set up for mid-market companies, piped to [the person] who handles that on our team. Additionally, reps get their own motions built for them. So all of that information is piped directly into slack for them. This dedicated motion means that accounts are fed and filtered and scored by intent, firmographics, behavior, etc. They have the option to even go ahead and build out linkedin connections and sort of a social selling motion directly from sift against all of these companies taking action on the website. The coolest thing beyond that: now it's a real time system built for multi channel action — but that's kind of table stakes at this point. Knowing that an account visited our website and what pages they went to doesn't tell us why we should be reaching out. So the second C, contextualize, comes into place here. The MCP that sift has built with claude allows us to add context to the data we're capturing. So right here I pulled a few sample accounts. What it's showing is things like web signals around M&A, new hires, exec changes, what might be happening in the industry right now, and a suggested hook or angle for our reps to latch on to as that first point of contact. So they get the foundational content — those first few sentences — written for them: actual context behind what that person was doing on our website and why it might make sense to get in touch with them. So now, with sift and this buildout with claude, we've got the right timing, the right relevance, and then our own humans to add the context and nuance on top of that. So motions go from "I saw that you were on our website" to "hey, looks like you just overhauled your entire go to market team — tells me that you need pipeline yesterday. Here's how we've helped companies like you build and validate strategies before their next major board meeting." It really sets up the posture that we're not just a seller, you know, another face in a sea of cold outbound — we're actual consultants to the prospect. It's core to how we run all of our programs and how we're doing it for clients as well. So I highly recommend that you all take a look.
